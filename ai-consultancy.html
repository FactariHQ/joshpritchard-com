AI that does the work, built into your organization

Most owners I talk to know AI could take real weight off their team. Very few have anyone to help them build it. That is the job I am here for: I sit down with you, find the work worth handing off, build it with you, and leave your people running it.

Why me

I am not a technologist who discovered ABA. I am a behavior analyst and operator who has spent the last year rebuilding how my own companies run with AI — and finding out, the expensive way, what holds up on a Monday morning and what only looks good in a demo.

Inside our own operation that now means claims entered into state Medicaid and managed-care portals, missed-EVV corrections worked every afternoon, scheduled sessions checked against actual starts, prior authorizations tracked before they lapse, treatment plans audited against a QA rubric, interview scorecards tied to 90-day retention, and a Slack channel where staff ask for any of it in plain English. None of that replaced a clinician. It gave admin and clinical leaders back the hours they were spending on keystrokes.

The behavior analysis matters more than people expect. An AI workflow is a contingency like any other: if nobody is measuring the right thing, or the people around it are reinforced for working around it, it quietly dies. I build for adoption, not just for function.

How it runs

From "we should be using AI" to it running

The aim is working systems your team owns — not a strategy deck, and not a dependency on me.

  • 01

    Find the work worth handing off

    We walk through where your people's hours actually go: billing, authorizations, scheduling, EVV, intake, credentialing, hiring, reporting, QA. I rank it by hours saved, money recovered and risk, so we start where it pays.

  • 02

    Build the first workflows with you

    We build two or three real ones on your systems — your EHR, your portals, your Google or Microsoft workspace, your Slack or Teams — and run them against live work, not sample data.

  • 03

    Put the guardrails in

    Who approves what before it goes out the door, what gets logged, where protected health information can and cannot go, and what a human always checks. Speed without these is how you end up writing apology letters to a payer.

  • 04

    Hand it to your team

    Each workflow gets an owner on your staff, written instructions, and a measure that tells you whether it is earning its keep. Your people learn to run it, fix it and build the next one.

  • 05

    Keep going, or don't

    Some clients want me back for the next round of builds; some take it from there. Both are good outcomes.

Where it pays

What I usually help teams build

Specific to the ABA and behavioral health business, because that is where I have done it.

Revenue cycle

Billing prep with authorization and unit checks before anything is filed, claim entry into payer portals, eligibility verification, and a clean log of what was billed and what was collected.

Scheduling and attendance

Schedule-versus-actual checks, cancellation triage and replacement, EVV corrections, time-off coverage views, and family-facing schedule updates.

Clinical quality

Chart and treatment-plan audits against your own rubric, authorization tracking, and supervision prep — tools that support your BCBAs' judgment rather than substituting for it.

People

Structured interviews and scorecards, onboarding communication, training trackers, and the early-attrition signals most organizations never get around to measuring.

Leadership visibility

Dashboards and daily digests built from the systems you already pay for, so leaders see the handful of numbers that matter without asking anyone to pull a report.

Your team, asking for it

A simple way for staff to request a job in plain language — in Slack or Teams — with clear rules about what runs immediately and what waits for a leader's approval.

When it fits

The owners who call me about this

Typical
  • Your admin team is underwater and hiring more of them is not the answer.
  • You have bought AI tools, and nobody is using them.
  • Claims, auths or EVV are leaking money and you can see it but not stop it.

Practicalities

Most of this work happens remotely, alongside your team, on the tools you already have. Where it helps, I come on site. I work best with ABA and behavioral health organizations; the same approach carries to most service businesses with a heavy admin load, and I will tell you honestly if yours is not a fit.

Scope and fee are set on the first call, because helping a single-site practice automate its billing prep and rebuilding the back office of a multi-state group are not the same engagement. If I do not think the work would pay for itself, I will say so.
