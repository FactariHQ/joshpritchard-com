Selected writing

Six worth your time

The complete list is on the CV. These are the ones I would hand you first.

  • 01

    Issues with Private Practice

    Wine, B., & Pritchard, J. K. (2020). Journal of Organizational Behavior Management, 40(3–4), 170–185.

    What actually goes wrong when clinicians become owners. Reprinted in 2024 in the Handbook of Organizational Performance.

  • 02

    The Gender Pay Gap for Behavior Analysis Faculty

    Li, A., Gravina, N., Pritchard, J. K., & Poling, A. (2019). Behavior Analysis in Practice, 12(4), 743–746.

    We counted. The numbers were not flattering to anyone.

  • 03

    Using Scorecards and a Lottery to Improve the Performance of Behavior Technicians

    Griffin, M., Gravina, N. E., Matey, N., Pritchard, J., & Wine, B. (2019). Journal of Organizational Behavior Management, 39(3–4), 280–292.

    Two autism clinics, a scorecard, and a lottery. Cheap interventions that moved real performance.

  • 04

    The Licensing of Behavior Analysts: Protecting the Profession and the Public

    Hassert, D. L., Kelly, A. N., Pritchard, J. K., & Cautilli, J. D. (2008). Journal of Early and Intensive Behavior Intervention, 5(2), 8–19.

    Written before the first licensure act passed. I then went and helped pass one.

  • 05

    The Importance of Professional Discourse for the Continual Advancement of Practice Standards: The RBT® as a Case in Point

    Leaf, J. B., et al. (2021). Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders, 51(5), 1789–1801.

    Twenty authors arguing that the field should be allowed to argue with itself.

  • 06

    A decade later: Creating single-subject design graphs with Microsoft Excel 2007

    Pritchard, J. K. (2008). The Behavior Analyst Today, 9, 153–161.

    The most downloaded thing I have ever written, and a lesson in what practitioners actually need.

Speaking

What I will come and talk about

Conferences, state associations, graduate programs, and company all-hands. I will tailor any of these, and I would rather do a workshop than a keynote.

Artificial intelligence and the behavior analyst

What machine learning actually changes about measurement, supervision and clinical decision-making — and the several things it does not change at all.

OBM for people who did not sign up for it

Every clinician who becomes a supervisor or owner inherits a performance-management job they were never trained for. This is the crash course.

Supervision that produces good behavior analysts

Building training systems with feedback loops tight enough to change behavior, across in-person, hybrid and fully remote models.

Owning the thing you practice

The business of a clinical practice: rates, payers, staffing math, and the decisions that quietly determine whether the clinical standard holds.

Advocacy that works

What actually moved a state Medicaid agency off a 50% rate cut, and what any state association can copy.
